What you're looking at

  • The move you described is two tweens per box, not one. A single ease can't be "fast, then bouncy" — so you split it: a travel tween, then a settle tween that picks up where the first left off. That's the technique to remember.
  • Travel — ease: "none" over 1.2s, covering everything except the last 34px. Constant speed all the way in: a box that eases into its own collision looks like it stopped on its own and then got hit, which is the wrong story.
  • Settle — that final 34px on bounce.out. Short distance, long duration (1.6s), which is what makes it read as settling rather than travelling. Bounce never passes its resting spot, so the boxes never overlap.
  • Slow stagger — 2.1s between each box starting, so one lands before the next is halfway across.
  • The impact is a third tween: whatever gets hit squashes to 93% width for 0.14s and springs back (yoyo: true, repeat: 1 plays it forwards then reverses it). transformOrigin: "left center" pins its left edge so it compresses away from the blow rather than shrinking evenly.
  • Box one has nothing in front of it, so it hits the wall instead — the accent bar.
